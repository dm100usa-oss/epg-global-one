Evidence and Research on How Website Errors Affect Customer Trust

A Global Lingo survey of 1,029 adults in the United Kingdom found that 59% of respondents would not use a company with obvious grammar or spelling mistakes on its website or marketing materials. 82% would not use a company if its materials were poorly translated into English.

Customers usually do not explain why. They simply leave.

+

The survey was conducted by Global Lingo, and the results were published by the British business publication Real Business.

Frequently Asked Questions

Updated: October 2026

Why EPG Global ONE?

In one audit, EPG Global ONE covers the areas for which companies usually hire several separate specialists: a translator, a proofreader, a website QA specialist, and a marketing specialist. The client receives one complete picture instead of scattered comments, saves money and time, and immediately sees what should be fixed first.

We do not separate proofreading and comparison with the main version of the website into two different services. It is one audit and one price. In one audit, we evaluate language and grammar, signs of machine translation, meaning and tone, links and language switching, request forms, differences in prices, phone numbers, and figures across language versions, leftover template text, and outdated pages. Audits are carried out by native speakers: editors, proofreaders, and translators with experience working with websites. For every error found, we show where it is located and suggest the correct version.

Our database was built in 2026 based on website audits of more than 130 companies in different countries and contains more than 2,200 errors that reduce customer trust. It grows with every new audit, so our recommendations are based on what is actually appearing on websites today.

What is a free quick website review?

A free quick website review is a preliminary review of your website by our specialists. We look for visible errors and inconsistencies in website copy and translations, button and menu labels, links, contact forms, and other elements that may reduce customer trust.

After the review, we send a quick review report to the work email address provided. This is not a full website audit. It is a first step to understand whether there are problems that may be causing the company to lose customers. If such problems are found, we recommend continuing with a pilot audit of 5 key pages, including the Customer Trust in Website Score.

To request a review, you only need to provide the website address, work email, name, and job title.

How is an EPG Global ONE website audit different from the work of a translator or proofreader?

A translator or proofreader works mainly with text. EPG Global ONE audits the entire website: text, links, navigation between pages, request forms, language switching, and consistency of information across language versions. Even when the translation itself is good, errors appear on the live website: a button leads to the wrong page, part of the text remains in English, a link does not work, or different language versions show different prices. That is why EPG Global ONE audits the published website as the customer actually sees it.

What does a company receive after a website audit by EPG Global ONE?

After the audit, the company receives three documents from EPG Global ONE: an executive report with the Customer Trust in Website Score (CTWS) from 0 to 100, scores by criteria, and an expert conclusion, plus separate task lists for the website editor and web developer. For every error, the report shows where it is located, what the customer sees, why it matters, and how to fix it, and the error numbers match across all documents. EPG Global ONE recommends fixing first the errors that prevent a customer from submitting a request, then issues that reduce trust, and only after that minor problems.
